# Anti-Slop Without Visual Blandness

Anti-slop means intentional fit, not compulsory minimalism. Follow `SKILL.md` source authority and preserve supported brand expression.

## Audit four dimensions
| Dimension | Signals requiring justification |
| --- | --- |
| Visual | Unrelated gradients/glow/glass, random accents, excessive radius, decorative icon tiles, ornamental charts, competing type styles. |
| Structure | Everything-as-card, nested wrappers, vanity KPI grids, repeated identical sections, oversized marketing spacing in task-heavy UI. |
| Content | Generic filler, fake metrics/testimonials, redundant helper text, unsupported claims, labels unrelated to the domain. |
| Interaction | Filters without a task, competing CTAs, hover-only essentials, ambiguous icon actions, needless confirmation, invented functionality. |

For a suspect element ask: does it improve workflow, hierarchy, comprehension, feedback, brand recognition, or coherent visual storytelling? Is there source support? Keep justified expression; remove or refine unsupported treatment.

## Positive quality bar
- Structure fits the product and the actual content.
- Hierarchy remains legible without relying on decorative effects alone.
- Typography, imagery, spacing, color, and surfaces form one language.
- Common controls remain understandable and accessible.
- Brand personality survives beyond swapping a logo and accent color.
- Realistic content and relevant states do not break the layout.

Use cards for meaningful independent groups, not every region. Strong expression is legitimate when the brand/reference calls for it. Do not flatten editorial, playful, or immersive brands into generic neutral SaaS UI.

If a design feels generic, fix the composition, typography, imagery, and domain-specific hierarchy before adding ornaments. Do not manufacture novelty in every component.
